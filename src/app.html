CONSUMER UNIT GUIDE

How to build and wire a CU in ElecLab

1 — Place the Consumer Unit

Find Consumer Unit in the UK House Wiring section of the toolbar and click anywhere on the canvas to place it. The box shows a supply section on the left and MCB slots to the right.

2 — Configure in the Properties Panel

Click the CU to select it. The right-hand panel becomes a CU Configurator:

  • Toggle DP Isolator, RCD (choose sensitivity: 10 / 30 / 100 / 300 mA), and SPD (surge protection)
  • Each row in the MCB list has an amperage selector (6–63 A) and a circuit label
  • Use + Add MCB Circuit to add slots, ✕ to remove them
  • Hit ✓ Apply Configuration when done — this locks in the terminal dots

3 — Understand the Terminals

After applying, three types of coloured dots appear on the CU edges:

  • L Red dot — left edge, mid-height. This is the Live input. Wire your mains supply's L terminal here.
  • N Blue dot — bottom of the supply section. This is the Neutral bar. Wire the supply's N here, and all circuit N-returns here too.
  • L1 L2… Green dots — one per MCB, along the bottom edge. These are the circuit outputs. Wire each to the live side of its circuit.
The CU distributes power — it does not generate it. You must wire a Supply component to the L and N terminals for any current to flow.

4 — Wire Your Circuits

Press W or click the Wire tool, then click terminal dot → component → next component. A typical lighting circuit looks like:

Supply L → CU L-in  |  Supply N → CU N-bar → GND
CU MCB L1 → Switch → Ceiling Rose → back to CU N-bar

5 — Simulate

Click Simulate (or press Enter from the canvas). The solver runs Modified Nodal Analysis on your circuit and shows voltages, currents, and power in the Results panel.

If a circuit draws more current than the MCB's rated amps, that MCB trips — its terminal dot turns red and the properties panel shows TRIP next to it.

6 — Reset Tripped MCBs

Fix the fault (reduce load, increase MCB rating, or fix a short circuit), then click Reset Tripped MCBs in the properties panel and run Simulate again.

Tips

  • Press R while placing a component to rotate it
  • Press S to switch to Select mode, W for Wire mode, H to pan
  • Hold Space and drag to pan; scroll wheel to zoom
  • Select a component and press Delete to remove it
  • Ctrl+Z undoes the last action
  • Add a GND component — the simulator needs a ground reference to solve
